Here's how to do booking for a small business without hiring a receptionist. Pick one scheduling tool. List each service with its length and price. Block out the hours you can actually take, turn on automatic confirmations and reminders, and decide on deposits. Then put the same booking link on your website and Google Business Profile. Free plans exist from [Cal.com](https://cal.com/pricing) and [Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/appointments/pricing), so your main cost is setup time.

## What does a small business booking system actually need to do?

Four jobs. It shows real availability, collects the customer's details, and sends confirmations and reminders. And if you want, it takes a deposit or payment. Anything past that is a nice extra.

Most of the setup comes down to one choice: which of those jobs go to software, and which you keep.

Plan for phones. Pew Research Center's 2026 survey found [91% of U.S. adults own a smartphone](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/). Test every step of your booking flow on a phone before you touch a laptop.

Phone and text booking still works. The catch is that every booking waits on you picking up, and that's tough with your hands full on a job. Think of self-booking as a second door next to your phone number. Customers who like to call can keep calling.

## Step 1: Which kind of booking tool fits your business?

Two broad kinds. Which one fits depends on how many people take appointments and whether you already run cards at a counter.

Work alone and sell appointments, like consults, lessons, grooming or repairs? A single-user scheduler covers it. [Cal.com's pricing page](https://cal.com/pricing) lists a Free plan at $0 for one user with unlimited event types and calendars, email and SMS notifications, and Stripe and PayPal payments.

Got staff and take cards in person? A tool built around a point-of-sale system often fits better. [Square Appointments](https://squareup.com/us/en/appointments/pricing) has a Free plan for solo professionals with online booking and reminders. Its pricing page puts cancellation policies, no-show fees, multi-staff booking and waitlists on the paid Plus plan. Check that tier before you count on those features.

We won't crown a best tool. Pick the one that matches how you work today. One more check before you commit: is it a provider Google supports for the Bookings button in Step 6? You don't need that, but it's easier to know up front.

## Step 2: How should you set up your services and time slots?

Make one bookable service for each thing you actually sell. Give each a length, a price or a starting-at price, and a one-line description. A clear name and a real duration beat a vague slot called "Appointment" every time.

Then set the rules around those services:

- **Buffer time** between appointments for travel, cleanup or notes. If you cover rural Montana, drives between jobs can be long, so pad generously.
- **Minimum notice**, such as no same-day bookings, so nobody grabs a slot that starts in 20 minutes.
- **Booking window**, meaning how far ahead people can book.
- **Intake questions.** Ask only what you need to do the job. Every extra field is one more reason to quit halfway.

Publish only the hours you can really take. If Tuesday afternoons go to paperwork, leave them off.

## Step 3: How do you stop double-booking yourself?

Connect the booking tool to the calendar you already live in. Say you have a dentist appointment or your kid's game on there. It blocks the time automatically, and no customer can book over it.

Use one booking tool. Just one. Two systems pulling from the same calendar is how two customers end up holding the same 2 p.m. slot.

Then test it. Put a fake one-hour event on your personal calendar and check that the slot vanishes from your public booking page. Still showing? The calendar isn't connected right.

## Step 4: Which reminders actually cut no-shows?

Reminders work, and there's real research behind that. A [systematic review of 29 studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3188816/) found phone and SMS reminders cut missed hospital appointments by about 34% of the baseline rate on average. Manual phone calls did a bit better, around 39%. Automated reminders came in around 29%. The same review found a reminder the day before worked about as well as one sent a week out.

One honest caveat. That's healthcare research. It doesn't tell you what a dog groomer or a plumber will see, so we won't promise you a percentage. The direction is clear enough to act on.

Here's the setup we'd use. Send a confirmation email the moment someone books, then one text or email reminder 24 hours before. Put a reschedule link in both. Someone who can't make it is more likely to move the appointment if moving it takes one tap.

## Step 5: Should you take a deposit, and what does that cost?

Deposits make sense for long appointments, services where people tend to skip out, or jobs where you buy materials ahead of time. For a quick, cheap service, skip them. The extra step can cost you more bookings than the occasional no-show costs you money.

The fees are small. [Stripe's U.S. rate](https://stripe.com/pricing) is 2.9% plus 30¢ per successful domestic online card charge. A $100 prepayment runs about $3.20.

Write your cancellation and refund policy on the booking page itself, in plain words. How much notice you need. What happens to the deposit. How to reschedule. A policy someone read before paying is a lot easier to stand behind than one they hear about after.

## Step 6: How do you add booking to your Google Business Profile?

Google offers two routes, and they're different things.

Option A is a plain booking link. Google's [help page on local business links](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6218037) says you can add up to 10 links per category. In Google Search, open Booking (in Maps, look under Edit profile), select Add link, paste your scheduler's URL and save. It works with any tool that gives you a link.

Option B is the provider Bookings button. Per Google's [provider setup page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7475773), you sign up through a supported provider from the Bookings section of your profile. Providers can take about a week to show up, and they may charge you for it.

Want to know how many bookings come from your profile? Point the link at a booking page on your own site and add UTM tags. Those are short tracking labels at the end of a URL that tell your analytics where a visitor came from.

Most owners should start with Option A. Haven't verified a profile yet? Start with [our guide to getting a Google Business Profile](/blog/how-to-get-google-business-profile/).

## Step 7: Where should the booking link live on your website?

Put a Book now button in your header and on every service page. Don't bury it on the Contact page under a paragraph about your history. Someone reading about a service and ready to commit should see the button right there.

Next, choose between embedding and linking out. Embedding puts the scheduler on a dedicated /book page, so people stay on your site with your photos and reviews around them. Linking out sends them to the tool's own page. It's simpler, and there's less to break. Either one is fine.

Before you tell anyone, run a test booking on your phone. Book it, cancel it, then book and reschedule it. If anything feels clunky to you, it'll feel worse to a customer.
