Do I need a website for my small business? Usually yes, but rarely a big one. If people search for your service and town, compare you with competitors, or need to book or request quotes, a simple site you own pays off. A cash-only, walk-in shop with a complete Google Business Profile can wait. Google shut down its free profile websites, so that fallback is gone.

## Who can honestly skip a website for now?

Some businesses can. Picture a walk-in shop or a route-based service. You're booked solid from referrals, and your Google Business Profile is claimed, verified and filled out. A website can wait a season. Spend the money somewhere else.

A profile alone can get you into Maps results. Google says local results rank mainly on [relevance, distance and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). Prominence is how well known you are. Google counts how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have, so a complete profile with steady reviews covers part of it. The links part is where a site helps.

The catch is control. Google also says there's [no way to request or pay for a better local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en), so you can't buy your way up the list. And a profile is a listing on Google's platform, not a website. Google sets the layout, the rules and what shows. You fill in the boxes.

If that sounds like you, skip the site for now and spend an afternoon [setting up your Google Business Profile properly](/blog/how-to-get-google-business-profile/). Come back when referrals slow down or you start taking bookings.

## What changed when Google stopped hosting free business websites?

For years, Google let you build a basic website right inside your Business Profile. Then it pulled the plug. [Search Engine Roundtable reported the shutdown](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-business-profiles-websites-now-shut-down-36999.html) as it happened in March 2024.

If that was your only website, your web address went with it. That's the real lesson. Free pages on someone else's platform can vanish when the platform changes its mind. A domain you own and a site you control keep your address yours.

There's a practical side too. When you add a website to a Business Profile, Google asks for the [full address, including http:// or https://](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7178589?hl=en), and says to make sure Googlebot isn't blocked from the site. A real site gives your profile somewhere to send people.

## Do your customers actually look you up online first?

Phone ownership doesn't prove anyone is searching for you. It does mean they can. Pew Research Center found that [91% of U.S. adults own a smartphone](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/), in a survey fielded January 30 to June 18, 2026. And [16% are smartphone-only internet users](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/), with no home broadband. So build for the phone first. Tap to call. Hours near the top. No pinching to zoom.

Rural numbers matter for a lot of Montana businesses. Still, if your customers live well outside Missoula or Bozeman, plenty of them will meet you on a small screen.

Retailers get one more number. E-commerce was an estimated [$347.3 billion, or 17.9% of total U.S. retail sales, in Q3 2026](https://www.census.gov/retail/ecommerce.html), seasonally adjusted. That's a retail figure. If you fix furnaces or cut hair, it doesn't apply to you. If you sell products and someone outside your store wants one, it's a real question.

## Which yes/no questions decide it for your business?

Answer these eight honestly. Give yourself one point for every yes.

| # | Question | Why it counts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do people search for your service plus your town, not just your name? | Google ranks local results on [relevance, distance and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). Strangers comparing options need more than a listing to pick you. |
| 2 | Would local partners, directories or press link to you if you had a site? | Google counts [links from other websites](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) toward prominence. |
| 3 | Is a Google profile or social page your only presence online? | A profile or social page is a listing on someone else's platform, not a site you control. |
| 4 | Do most customers find you from their phones? | [91% of U.S. adults own a smartphone](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/). |
| 5 | Do you serve rural customers? | [20% of rural U.S. adults](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/) depend on their phone for internet. |
| 6 | Do you take bookings or quote requests? | Your profile's website field needs a [full URL to send people to](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7178589?hl=en). |
| 7 | Do you sell products to people who aren't in the store? | E-commerce was [17.9% of U.S. retail sales in Q3 2026](https://www.census.gov/retail/ecommerce.html). |
| 8 | Do you need to explain prices, service areas, licenses or insurance? | Google [finds published pages on its own](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), so that information can show up in search. |

Now add it up.

| Score | Your path | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | Profile is enough for now | Finish your profile, collect reviews, check again in six months |
| 3 to 5 | One-page site | Build the small version described below. Add a lead form or booking if question 6 was a yes |
| 6 to 8 | Multi-page site with booking, forms or payments | Plan for more pages and the tools you need |

One override. A yes on question 7 puts you on the third path no matter your total, because selling online needs payments and more pages. Question 6 doesn't force that. A lead form or a booking tool can sit on a one-page site.

## If the answer is yes, what is the smallest site that works?

Smaller than most owners think. Five blocks cover it:

- What you do, in plain words
- Where you serve, by town or radius
- Your hours and phone number, with tap-to-call on mobile
- One clear way to contact you or book
- Proof you're legitimate: license, insurance, photos of real work

That's it. No blog, no animations, no slideshow.

You usually don't have to submit anything to Google, either. Its [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says Google finds most pages automatically with crawlers once a site is published.

Then tie the two together. Put your site's full URL in your Business Profile, and link back to the profile from your site so people can read your reviews. After that, our guide to [how local SEO works](/blog/how-does-local-seo-work/) covers what to do next.

## How do you create one without wasting money?

You've got three routes. Each one costs you something different.

A do-it-yourself builder is cheapest in cash. It costs your evenings, and every update after launch is yours too. If you like that kind of thing, go for it.

A one-time build means paying more upfront for a finished site. You own the result. Changes later fall back on you or turn into another invoice.

A managed monthly plan has a lower upfront cost, and someone else handles updates. You're committing to a term, though. That's what we sell through our [website design service](/services/website-design/), so weigh our opinion with that in mind.

Whichever route you pick, do it in this order:

1. Secure your domain name.
2. Write the five blocks above.
3. Publish the site.
4. Add the full URL to your Business Profile.
5. Add booking, forms or payments only if the checklist called for them.

Scored a 2? Skip all of this and go finish your profile. That's the whole job this week.
