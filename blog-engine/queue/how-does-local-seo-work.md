How does local SEO work? [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) it ranks local results mainly on three signals. Relevance: does your business match the search? Distance: how close are you to the searcher, or to the place they named? Prominence: how well known are you, based on reviews, links and mentions? Your profile, website and reviews prove the first and third. And you can't pay Google for a better local ranking.

## What are the three signals Google uses to rank local results?

[Google's help page on local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) spells them out. Relevance is how well your Business Profile matches what someone searched for. Distance is how far each possible result is from the location the searcher used. Prominence is how well known you are.

Google combines all three. So a shop across town can outrank the one down the street if Google judges it the better match. Google doesn't publish how much each signal counts, and it [states plainly](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) that businesses can't request or pay for a better local ranking. Anyone guaranteeing you a top map spot is promising something Google doesn't sell.

Quick definitions, since the terms get blurred. SEO is the work of showing up in Google's standard web results. Local SEO is the same idea plus the map results and Google Maps, where your profile, location and reviews do most of the talking. Serve people in one area? You're playing both games at once. ## Which checklist items help Google see you as relevant?

Relevance rewards being specific. [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) complete, detailed business information helps it match your profile to the right searches. A half-filled profile, with missing hours, a thin description and services you never got around to adding, gives it a lot less to work with. Fill in every field.

| Add LocalBusiness structured data, test it, submit a sitemap | Relevance | [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) | One-time, then spot checks | Your time / ask |

Our [step-by-step Google Business Profile guide](/blog/how-to-get-google-business-profile/) walks through claiming and verifying. Keep your profile name the same as the name on your sign. That's the name customers will recognize on the map.

Structured data sounds technical. Think of it as the label on the back of a jar: a bit of code on your site that tells Google your hours, departments and reviews in a format it reads directly. [Google recommends](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) validating it with the Rich Results Test, checking the live page with the URL Inspection tool and submitting a sitemap. Set it up once. Check it now and then.

## What can you control about distance, and what can't you?

You can't move closer to the searcher. What you control is whether your location data is accurate.

Start with one question. Do customers come to you? A bakery or a salon shows its address. A business with no storefront that travels to customers is allowed one service-area profile under [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en). If you don't serve customers at your address, [Google says to hide it](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137?hl=en), and your profile then shows only your service area.

| Task | Signal | Source | One-time or ongoing | Cost: DIY / Mayfly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hide your address if customers don't come to you | Distance | [Google: service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137?hl=en) | One-time | Your time / ask |
| Keep your service area within about 2 hours' drive of your base | Distance | [Google: service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137?hl=en) | One-time, review yearly | Your time / ask |
| Skip PO boxes and addresses where you don't actually serve customers | Distance | [Google: representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) | One-time | Your time / ask |

Montana makes the two-hour guideline interesting. [Google recommends](https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137?hl=en) keeping your overall service area within about 2 hours' driving time of where you're based. Out here, two hours can mean a mountain pass and a different valley. So pick your towns on purpose. List the places you actually want work from and can still reach in January, when an icy pass can turn a quick job in the next valley into most of a day. Not every dot on the map.

## How do reviews, links and mentions build prominence?

Prominence is reputation, measured. Reviews are the part you can work on every week.

| Task | Signal | Source | One-time or ongoing | Cost: DIY / Mayfly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ask every customer for a review, happy or not | Prominence | [Google Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) | Ongoing | Your time / ask |
| Never offer payment, discounts or freebies for reviews, or for removing a bad one | Prominence | [Google Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) | Ongoing rule | Free to follow |
| Verify your profile, then reply to reviews politely with no private details | Prominence | [Google: reply to reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9075681) | Ongoing | Your time / ask |
| Earn links and mentions from local directories, news and organizations | Prominence | [Google: local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) | Ongoing | Your time / ask |

The review rules are strict. [Google Maps policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) bans payment, discounts and free goods or services in exchange for a review. It also bans discouraging negative reviews and selectively asking only happy customers. So no "10% off for five stars." No refund in exchange for deleting a one-star. No exceptions.

Replies need a verified profile. Keep replies short. Thank people. Own the problem when there is one. Never mention what someone bought or said privately.

Links and mentions build slower. Think chamber of commerce listings, a local news story about something you actually did, a sponsor page for a youth team, or a trade association directory that lists its members by town.

## Should you be listed beyond Google?

Yes. It's mostly one-time work, and it reaches people who don't search on Google. [Microsoft redesigned Bing Places for Business](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/microsoft-launches-new-bing-places-for-business/557520/), which now lives at bing.com/forbusiness and includes an option to import your existing Google Business Profile. If your Google profile is complete, that import saves most of the typing. Claim Apple Business Connect too, for people searching in Apple Maps.

| Task | Signal | Source | One-time or ongoing | Cost: DIY / Mayfly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claim Bing Places and import your Google profile | Beyond Google | [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/microsoft-launches-new-bing-places-for-business/557520/) | One-time | Your time / ask |
| Claim Apple Business Connect for Apple Maps | Beyond Google | Apple's own tool (not a Google source) | One-time | Your time / ask |
| Keep your name, address and phone number identical everywhere | Housekeeping | Common practice | Ongoing | Your time / ask |

Keeping your name, address and phone the same on every listing is basic housekeeping. It saves customers from calling an old number or driving to an old address. It's not a secret trick. ## Which checklist items do you repeat every month?

Local SEO isn't a project you finish. Your hours shift with the seasons, customers keep leaving reviews and other businesses keep updating their profiles. Here's the short recurring list:

- Reply to every new review, good or bad.
- Update your hours for holidays and seasonal changes, like winter hours or a summer schedule.
- Add a few fresh photos and a post to your Business Profile.
- Run your key pages through the [Rich Results Test](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) and check Search Console for errors.
- Glance at your Bing and Apple listings to make sure they still match Google.
