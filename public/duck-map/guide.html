Montana duck guide 2026–27

Built from our data analysis and ~25 sources. REAL measured birds · MODELED estimate · INF our reasoning from the evidence.

The big picture

  • Most fall mallards come down from Alberta and Saskatchewan REAL. Fall comes in two waves: a late-October pulse on prairie water, then a December build-up on open water MODELED.
  • Birds stop farther north than they used to: mallard band recoveries moved ~110 mi north from 1960 to 2019, and more prairie mallards now head to WA/OR REAL.
  • 2026 breeding mallards: 5.9 million continent-wide (−11%), and −33% in the western Dakotas + Montana REAL. Expect fewer local birds early INF.
  • Mallards are 41–65% of Montana's duck harvest each year; teal, wigeon and gadwall are growing REAL.

When the birds move: weather rules

From 147 real mallard migrations (USGS, ND/SD, same latitude as the Hi-Line). Our analysis.

  1. Watch Alberta and Saskatchewan. Their first hard freeze sends new birds south INF.
  2. The morning after the first night well below 14 °F with a north or northwest wind, look for fresh birds on open water south of the cold, and expect frozen potholes to be empty INF.
  3. Once small water locks (late Oct to early Nov on the prairie), birds pile onto rivers, tailwaters, spring-fed water and big lakes.
  4. Warm, calm spells: birds sit tight and just commute between roost and field.

Region by region

Great Falls is in the Pacific Flyway (closes Jan 15). Early season: Freezout and Benton Lake. Late season: the Missouri River through and below town (peaks early January in the model) INF.

A mallard's day

  • Roost on big open water or a closed refuge; feed in harvested grain, usually 1–3 miles away, sometimes 12–20 REAL (GPS studies elsewhere).
  • Barley is preferred over wheat; more snow means longer, later feeding flights REAL.
  • Under pressure, birds sit on no-hunt water by day and feed at night REAL. Hunt the field lanes between big roosts and the nearest stubble INF.
  • Migration flights happen mostly at night, at 4,000–5,000 ft, on tailwinds. One bird covered ~600 miles in a night.

Other ducks

  • Blue-winged teal leave early. Tracked Saskatchewan teal crossed Montana's south edge by a median of Oct 12 REAL.
  • Green-winged teal, wigeon, gadwall are a growing share of the harvest. Their timing data isn't on the map yet.
  • Fort Peck holds divers (scaup, redheads) plus teal and wigeon early in the fall.
