# Montana ducks besides mallards

Harvest (REAL survey, S17; Montana totals have ±21–42% error):
| Year | Total ducks | Mallards | Mallard share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 169,800 | 109,744 | 65% |
| 2019 | 104,600 | 63,247 | 60% |
| 2020 | 218,700 | 127,437 | 58% |
| 2022 | 79,300 | 37,479 | 47% |
| 2023 | 134,700 | 55,246 | 41% |
Mallards are always #1, but green-winged teal, wigeon and gadwall make up a growing share (2023: GWT 18,020, wigeon 12,804).

Timing:
- Blue-winged teal (REAL, our analysis of S3): Saskatchewan-tagged teal crossed south of 45°N (Montana's south edge) on a
  median **Oct 12** (range Sep 2 – Oct 22, n = 11). Most leave before late November. They're gone in the late season.
- Blue-winged teal leave breeding areas Aug–Sep because shallow-wetland food freezes first (DU, secondary).
- Mallard and pintail migrations start earlier than in the 1960s, and both stop farther north (S15).
- Fort Peck: divers (scaup, redheads) plus teal and wigeon in early fall (secondary).
- No public Montana data on wigeon, gadwall, pintail or goldeneye timing found. eBird S&T has these species; adding them
  (gadwal, amewig, norpin, gnwtea, comgol codes) is the next species step (needs EBIRDST_KEY on Mitch's laptop).
