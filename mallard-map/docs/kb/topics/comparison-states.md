# Learning from places with more data (transfer to Montana)

Montana has no public mallard GPS tracks, so we borrow from places that are similar in latitude, landscape or birds.
Each row says what transfers and how much to trust it.

| Place | Why it's comparable | What it shows | Transfer to Montana | Trust |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North & South Dakota (USGS, 97 GPS juveniles 2018–20; S2, S14) | Same latitude as the Hi-Line/NE MT; prairie pothole + Missouri River reservoirs, like Fort Peck–NE | Long migrations are weather-triggered (cold nights, snow, tailwind); short moves are not; heading ~SSE; distances 300–1,800 km | Direct for eastern MT (Central Flyway). Use the departure rules in weather-triggers-data.md | High (east), medium (west) |
| Alberta & Saskatchewan (band recoveries, DU Canada; S15, S16, S21) | Directly upstream: MT's fall birds come largely from AB/SK; same grain-prairie landscape | Migration starting earlier; birds stopping farther north ("short-stopping", ~180 km shift 1960–2019); more prairie mallards using the Pacific Flyway (to WA/OR); SK freeze-up ~late Nov; southern AB rivers hold big mallard flocks after ponds lock | Expect MT's late season to hold more birds than decades ago; birds staged in AB/SK arrive in MT with the first hard freeze up north | Medium–high |
| Washington / Oregon / Idaho (Pacific Flyway; S16, DU) | Destination for west-of-Divide and many prairie mallards; mountain valleys + irrigated ag like western MT | Prairie mallards in the Pacific Flyway winter in WA/OR; Columbia Basin is the big inland wintering area | Western MT (Flathead, Bitterroot, Missoula) birds likely exit W/SW toward ID/WA — matches the model's west exits | Medium (no GPS in ID found) |
| Wyoming / Colorado (WGFD) | Same Rockies + open-river winter pattern; Bighorn Basin continues the Bighorn River | Dependable winter habitat is limited; January mallards use shallow open water and grain fields | Supports the Bighorn/Yellowstone 'late season' class | Low–medium (no tracking) |
| Nebraska (wintering mallard diet; S22) | Cold, grain-dominated winter range | Waste corn 97% of ag diet; flight distance to fields grows with snow cover; competition rises in cold, snowy winters | MT has more barley/wheat than corn; grain-choice studies in SK show barley preferred | Medium |
| Tennessee / Mississippi Valley / California (GPS; S19, S20) | Many birds and hunters, lots of GPS | Daily moves mostly 1–5 km, some 20–100 km; mallards sit on closed refuges by day and feed off-refuge at night under pressure; fewer flights in cold weather | Behavior, not geography: expect birds on no-hunt water by day, flights to fields at dawn/dusk, less flying in extreme cold | Medium |
| Europe (Sweden, Netherlands GPS; S19) | Lots of mallard GPS; cold winters in Sweden | Roost-to-field 1–5 km typical, up to 26 km | Confirms 'birds feed within a few miles of the roost' | Medium |

Bottom line: the Dakotas tell us **when** birds leave (weather), the Canadian prairies tell us **when birds arrive and
from where**, the Pacific Northwest tells us **where western MT birds go**, and the GPS studies elsewhere tell us **how
birds use a day** (roost ↔ field) and respond to hunting pressure.
