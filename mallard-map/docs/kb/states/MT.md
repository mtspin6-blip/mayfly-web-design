## Where mallards are in duck season (MODELED, S1) — full list: topics/hotspots.md
| Region | Strongest areas (tier) | Peak |
|---|---|---|
| Flathead | North shore of Flathead Lake / Flathead WPA (High, #1 in MT); lake near Kalispell and Polson (High); Flathead River near Ronan (Medium) | mid-Dec |
| Fort Peck–NE | Fort Peck Lake and the dredge cuts (5 High cells); Medicine Lake area (High) | late Oct (dredge cuts) to late Dec (open lake) |
| Billings–Yellowstone | Bighorn River near Fort Smith (High, #4 in MT); Yellowstone valley near Laurel/Billings (Medium) | mid-Dec |
| Hi-Line | Fort Peck dredge cuts, Nelson Reservoir, Fresno Reservoir (late Oct peaks: fall migrants) | late Oct |
| Great Falls | Freezout Lake WMA, Missouri River in Great Falls, Sun River near Augusta, Benton Lake NWR (all Medium) | Oct (Benton) → Jan (Missouri) |
| Helena–Bozeman / SW | Gallatin River near Belgrade (High), Canyon Ferry, Lake Helena, Ennis Lake, Beaverhead River (Medium) | Nov–Dec; some Mar |
| Bitterroot | Bitterroot River (Hamilton, Stevensville, Lolo) and Clark Fork at Missoula (all Low) | rising all winter, peak ~Mar 1 |

Pattern (MODELED, S1): reservoirs and prairie potholes peak in late October (migrants passing through); rivers, tailwaters,
spring-fed valleys and big lakes that stay open peak mid-Dec to early Jan (birds that stay until freeze-up). This
matches hunter reports that late-season mallards pack onto open river water (S9) — **secondary** support.

## Bitterroot (answering "is there no flight path there?")
- MODELED (S1): every Bitterroot cell is Low tier in season (season avg ≤ 8 vs 166 at Flathead's north shore), and
  numbers keep rising after the season closes (peak ~Mar 1). So the valley holds a modest, growing winter population.
- MODELED paths: since 2026-11-28 the model gives every region a hub. Bitterroot gets thin paths: in from the north (west
  of the Divide) and out south over Lost Trail Pass; Flathead birds exit west via Lolo Pass. Shares are < 1% of state
  movement, which is why they were filtered out before.
- REF (S8): Lee Metcalf NWR hosts 20+ waterfowl species in migration with no big fall waves.

## Real tracked birds (REAL)
- Mallards: no public GPS tracks in Montana (checked Movebank public, Movebank Data Repository, USGS; 2026-11-27).
- Blue-winged teal (S3): 3 of 42 birds had fixes in MT, mostly NE corner; most teal leave before late November.
