# Montana duck hunting and movement: expert synthesis (2026–27)

Built 2026-11-28 from our data analyses and ~25 sources (see `sources.md`). Tags: **REAL** measured birds · **MODELED**
estimate · **REG** regulation · **INF** our inference from the evidence (reasoned, not tested). Numbers in
"Season type" and "Share" come from `topics/regional-timing.md` (eBird S&T v2025, MODELED).

## 1. The big picture
- **Montana is a pass-through and short-stop state.** Its fall mallards come mostly from Alberta and Saskatchewan
  (REAL, band recoveries, S15/S16). Fall has two waves: a **late-October pulse** of migrants on prairie water, then a
  **December build-up** of birds that stay on open water until it freezes (MODELED, regional-timing.md).
- **Birds are staying farther north than they used to.** Mallard band recoveries moved ~180 km north from 1960–2019,
  and more prairie mallards now use the Pacific Flyway to WA/OR (REAL, S15/S16). INF: Montana's late season holds more
  birds than older hunters' rules of thumb suggest.
- **2026 breeding numbers are down:** 5.9 M mallards continent-wide (−11% vs 2025, −26% vs long-term), and breeding
  mallards in the western Dakotas + Montana −33% (REAL survey, S23). INF: fewer local birds early; the late season will
  lean on northern migrants and weather.
- **Mallards are the main bird:** 41–65% of Montana's duck harvest each year (REAL, S17), with teal, wigeon and gadwall growing.

## 4. Weather: when birds leave and arrive (REAL, our analysis of 147 departures, `topics/weather-triggers-data.md`)
- A night at **−10 °C (14 °F) or colder** was **6.6x** more common on departure days; −15 °C (5 °F): **13x**.
- **Snow on the ground** (≥ 1 cm): 2.6x. **Tailwind:** 85% of departures (vs 59% of other days).
- Short local moves (< 310 km) did **not** follow weather; long migrations did.
- Published model (S14): initial migration driven most by minimum temperature + favorable wind; each 1 cm of snow
  raised the chance of a later migration 80%; colder = farther. Literature threshold for mallards: WSI ≈ 7.2–8 (S18).
- Flights are mostly at night, high (4,000–5,000 ft), on tailwinds; a bird can cover ~600 miles in a night (S24).

**Hunter's rule (INF from the above):**
1. Watch Alberta/Saskatchewan: their first hard freeze (late Oct–Nov; SK freeze-up ~late Nov, S21) sends new birds south.
2. The morning after the **first night well below 14 °F with a north/northwest wind** in or north of your area,
   expect fresh birds on open water south of the cold — and empty potholes where it froze.
3. Once small water locks (late Oct–early Nov on the prairie, S21), birds pile onto rivers, tailwaters, spring-fed
   water and big lakes — that's when Flathead, the Bighorn, the Missouri and the Gallatin shine.
4. Warm, calm spells: birds sit tight; local roost ↔ field movement only.

## 5. A mallard's day (REAL from other states; transfers well — S19, S20, S22)
- Roost on big open water or closed refuge; feed in harvested grain (barley preferred over wheat) usually **1–5 km**
  away, sometimes 20–30 km. More snow = longer, later feeding flights.
- Under pressure, birds sit on no-hunt water by day and feed at night. INF: hunt field lanes between big roosts and the
  nearest barley/wheat stubble, and give closed areas a buffer.
- Extreme cold reduces flights; a few days of extreme cold followed by open water = birds leaving.

## 6. Other ducks (see `topics/species.md`)
- Blue-winged teal: gone early — tracked teal crossed Montana's south edge by a median of **Oct 12** (REAL, our analysis).
- Green-winged teal, wigeon, gadwall: a growing share of the harvest (REAL S17); timing data for MT not yet loaded.
- Divers (scaup, redhead) and teal use Fort Peck in early fall (secondary).
